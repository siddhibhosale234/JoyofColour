Chapter II · The Aesthetics of Growing Up

Why did bright colour become a ‘brave choice’?

When someone paints their front door flamingo pink or buys a cobalt kettle, the standard adult compliment is rarely “that looks joyful”. More often, it is a slightly hesitant: “Ooh, how brave!”

That single adjective gives the game away. Minimalism trained us to equate muted neutrality with safety, competence, and good taste. Stepping outside oatmeal and slate began to feel like taking a social risk—as though wearing magenta required courage rather than simply liking pink.

Simplicity has real value when it clears away clutter. Yet somewhere along the way, we mistook visual calm for emotional silence, scrubbing away the playful hues that once anchored our everyday delight.

Chapter IV · Bringing Fun Back Home

A field guide to taking colour less seriously

Principle 01

Start with low-stakes objects

You do not have to repaint your entire hallway tomorrow. Begin with a bright pink toaster, mismatched stripy mugs, or neon sticky notes that make your desk feel alive.

Principle 02

Stop decorating for future strangers

Resale neutrality turns homes into waiting rooms. Let your space reflect who actually wakes up there—not an imaginary buyer five years from now.

Principle 03

Appreciation meets celebration

Simplicity and colour are not enemies. You can appreciate clean lines while still celebrating joyful, unapologetic pink and rainbow moments.
